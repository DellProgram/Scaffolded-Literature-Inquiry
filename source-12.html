Part IV · Who Gets to Define a Legacy?

Source 12 of 14

An Authentic History of the Douglass Monument

Published eight years after Frederick Douglass’s death, John W. Thompson’s book combines biography with an account of the effort to commemorate Douglass through a public monument. It gives us evidence not only about how Douglass was described, but also about how people in the early twentieth century chose to preserve his legacy.

Source Context

Commemoration is also interpretation.

This book was created after Douglass’s lifetime, so it is not a firsthand record of everything he experienced. Instead, Thompson looks backward and makes choices about which events, accomplishments, and qualities of Douglass deserve emphasis.

How to Analyze It

Author

Remember that Thompson is writing after Douglass’s death.

Purpose

Connect the biography to the effort to build a monument.

Compare

Test the commemoration against Douglass’s own words.

Selected Passage

Read the author's purpose before answering the questions.

In the introduction, Thompson explains that he is preserving the history of the Douglass monument along with biographical information about Douglass. He presents the monument as a form of public memory meant to influence later generations.

“Its history will be an inspiration for generations to come, inciting American manhood to love of country”

Guiding Questions

Analyze how Douglass’s life becomes public memory.

Use the book’s language, organization, and purpose as evidence rather than treating the account as a neutral summary.

  1. 1

    Identify what is emphasized.

    What parts of Frederick Douglass’s life or accomplishments does this source emphasize? Which details seem to be presented as most important?

  2. 2

    Consider the purpose of commemoration.

    Why do you think Thompson connected Douglass’s life story with the creation of a monument to him? What does that suggest about how people wanted future generations to remember Douglass?

  3. 3

    Compare remembrance with self-representation.

    Compare this book with one of Douglass’s own speeches or writings. How is Douglass’s legacy presented differently when he is speaking for himself versus when someone else is commemorating him after his death? Use evidence from both sources.
