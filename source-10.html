Death / Memorial “Prayer”

Up to this point, you have heard Frederick Douglass speak for himself and seen how others represented him during his lifetime. This handwritten memorial document shifts the inquiry toward remembrance: what did people choose to emphasize after Douglass’s death?

Source Context

A memorial tells us as much about the people remembering as the person being remembered.

This document belongs to the group of materials preserved in the Frederick Douglass Papers concerning his death and remembrance. Unlike Douglass’s autobiography or speeches, this source does not give us Douglass’s own explanation of his life. Instead, it shows how another writer chose to frame his meaning and legacy.

How to Analyze It

Language

Notice repeated moral or religious words.

Purpose

Decide how the writer wants Douglass remembered.

Compare

Compare this memory with Douglass’s own voice.

Guiding Questions

Study how Douglass is remembered.

Read for emphasis, perspective, and omission. Then compare this memorial voice with Douglass’s own voice from earlier sources.

  1. 1

    Identify what is emphasized.

    What words, phrases, or ideas does the writer use to describe Frederick Douglass or the meaning of his life? What qualities receive the most attention?

  2. 2

    Interpret the memorial.

    Based on this document, how did the writer want people to remember Frederick Douglass? What evidence from the source supports your interpretation?

  3. 3

    Compare perspectives.

    Compare the Frederick Douglass remembered in this document with the Douglass you encountered in one of his own speeches or writings. What is emphasized, left out, or presented differently? Why might that matter when historians study someone’s legacy?
