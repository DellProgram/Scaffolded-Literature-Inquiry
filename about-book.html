Frederick Douglass:
The Lion Who Wrote History

This picture-book biography is the starting point. We will use it to establish the major events and ideas in Frederick Douglass's life before testing, expanding, and complicating that story with historical evidence.

Author
Walter Dean Myers
Illustrator
Floyd Cooper
Published
2017

Why We Start Here.

The book introduces Frederick Douglass through a clear narrative that emphasizes the importance of literacy, freedom, and his continuing work for the rights of Americans. It gives us a shared starting point before we begin working with more difficult nineteenth-century documents and images.

A biography, however, is still a secondary source. An author and illustrator must choose which events to include, which details to emphasize, and how to represent a person's life. As historians, our job is to ask what the book helps us understand—and what questions it leaves for the primary sources.

Initial Reflection

These are starting ideas, not final conclusions. Use this so you can return to them after examining the historical evidence.

  1. Question 1

    Which event or experience in the book seems most important in shaping Frederick Douglass? Explain why.

  2. Question 2

    What relationship does the book suggest between literacy and freedom?

  3. Question 3

    What is one question about Douglass, freedom, or citizenship that the book leaves you wanting to investigate further?
