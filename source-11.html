The Republican: “In Memory of the Hon. Frederick Douglass”

This memorial newspaper page shows how Frederick Douglass was presented to a public audience after his death. Its headline, images, page layout, and selected stories all help reveal what the editors believed readers should remember about him.

Guiding Questions

Analyze how the newspaper constructs a public image of Douglass.

Use both the written content and the visual organization of the page as evidence.

  1. 1

    Read the page as a design.

    What do you notice first about the newspaper’s headline, images, layout, and amount of space devoted to Frederick Douglass? What do these choices suggest about how important the editors considered him?

  2. 2

    Identify what is remembered.

    What accomplishments, qualities, or parts of Douglass’s life does the newspaper choose to emphasize? What picture of Douglass would a reader receive from these choices?

  3. 3

    Compare public memory with Douglass’s own voice.

    Compare this newspaper’s portrayal of Douglass with the Douglass you encountered in his autobiography or speeches. Does the newspaper capture the complexity of his ideas and activism, or does it simplify or emphasize certain parts of his story? Use evidence from both sources.
