Guiding Questions

Evaluate the story Johnson tells about Douglass.

Use both Johnson’s words and the primary evidence you have already examined. A secondary source is an interpretation—not the final word.

  1. 1

    Identify Johnson’s emphasis.

    How does Kathryn M. Johnson describe Frederick Douglass and the larger struggle against slavery? What words or ideas show what she believes is most important about this history?

  2. 2

    Evaluate perspective and distance.

    Because Johnson wrote about Douglass after his death, how might her purpose and time period influence the way she tells his story? What information would you want to compare with a primary source before accepting her interpretation?

  3. 3

    Test the biography against primary evidence.

    After examining Douglass’s own writings, speeches, and the other primary sources, how well does Johnson’s portrayal represent the Frederick Douglass you have encountered? What does she emphasize, and what does she leave out or simplify? Use evidence from this source and at least one primary source.
