Source Context

What kind of evidence is a map?

A map can show location, distance, waterways, roads, communities, and other features of a place. It can help historians understand the physical setting in which people lived and moved.

How to Analyze It

Locate

Identify places, waterways, roads, and other features.

Infer

Use the map to make a claim about movement or place.

Limit

Decide what the map cannot tell you about Douglass’s experiences.

Guiding Questions

Use the map as evidence.

Answer each question with specific details from the source. Avoid making claims the map cannot support.

  1. 1

    Observe the setting.

    What places, waterways, roads, and other geographic features do you notice on the map?

  2. 2

    Make an evidence-based inference.

    What does this map suggest about how people may have traveled between communities in Talbot County? Use details from the map as evidence.

  3. 3

    Evaluate the source.

    How does this map help you understand the setting of Frederick Douglass's early life? What does the map not tell us about his experiences?
