Portrait Photograph of Frederick Douglass

After several sources centered on speeches, politics, and public debate, this photograph asks you to examine a different kind of evidence. What can a posed portrait add to our understanding of Frederick Douglass—and what remains outside the frame?

Guiding Questions

Read the photograph as historical evidence.

Observe first, interpret second, and then compare this image with the public versions of Douglass shown in earlier sources.

  1. 1

    Observe carefully.

    What do you notice about Frederick Douglass, the other person, their clothing, their poses, and the objects included in the photograph?

  2. 2

    Interpret the image.

    What impression of Frederick Douglass does this photograph create? Choose two visual details that contribute to that impression and explain how.

  3. 3

    Compare and evaluate.

    How does this photograph add to or complicate the image of Douglass you have seen in previous sources? What can this photograph tell us that a speech or political cartoon might not—and what can it not tell us?
