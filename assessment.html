Final Assessment.

Create a argument that answers the essential question with a clear claim and evidence from the inquiry.

How did Frederick Douglass use his experiences, his words, and his public influence to challenge ideas about freedom and citizenship in the United States?

Use at least 3 sources · at least 2 primary sources · support one clear claim

Rubric

100 points total.

Historical Claim

Use of Evidence

Analysis, Context & Perspective
20 pts		 18–20
Explains creator, audience, purpose, context, perspective, and limitations when relevant. Clearly distinguishes what sources show from what the student infers.		 15–17
Analyzes perspective and context for most sources and moves beyond simple description.		 11–14
Includes some analysis, but frequently summarizes or treats sources as neutral statements of fact.		 0–10
Provides little source analysis or shows major misunderstanding of perspective or context.
Synthesis & Change Over Time
20 pts		 18–20
Connects evidence across sources to explain patterns, tensions, change over time, and at least one meaningful complication or limitation. Effectively incorporates the anchor book.		 15–17
Makes clear connections among sources, addresses change over time, and includes the anchor-book connection.		 11–14
Makes limited cross-source connections or addresses change over time and the anchor book only generally.		 0–10
Treats sources separately with little synthesis, or omits major required connections.
Communication, Design & Citations
15 pts		 14–15
Exhibit is clear, readable, purposeful, visually organized, and carefully edited. Source credits are complete and the design supports the historical argument.		 11–13
Exhibit is organized and readable with only minor errors or citation issues.		 8–10
Organization, readability, mechanics, or source credits are inconsistent and sometimes distract from the argument.		 0–7
Product is difficult to follow, incomplete, poorly edited, or missing significant source information.
